You may know exactly where a pattern came from. You may be able to explain why you shut down in conflict, why rest feels uncomfortable, or why closeness brings both longing and fear. And still, when the moment arrives, your whole system responds before thought has time to intervene.

This does not mean that insight has failed. It means that some forms of knowing live beyond words.

## The body’s version of the story

Our experience is organized not only through conscious thought, but through breath, posture, tension, impulse, expectation, and the protective responses that helped us adapt. These patterns can continue long after the circumstances that shaped them have changed.

Somatic work creates another pathway. Instead of asking only, “Why do I do this?” we might gently notice, “What happens inside as I say that?”

Perhaps the breath gets shallow. A shoulder turns slightly away. A familiar sentence appears: *I have to handle this alone.*

Nothing has to be corrected. We become curious.

## Change at the pace of safety

In Hakomi, present-moment experience can make unconscious beliefs more visible. When those beliefs are approached with enough support and choice, their emotional charge may begin to soften.

The aim is not to erase the past or guarantee a particular outcome. It is to create space for a response that once felt automatic to become a little less fixed, and for possibilities that were hard to imagine to become easier to see.

Sometimes understanding is the beginning. The body may help us take the next step.
