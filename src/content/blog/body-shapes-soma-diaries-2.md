“Boy, you’re gonna carry that weight, carry that weight a long time…” Golden Slumbers, The Beatles.

“Loosen loosen Baby, you don’t have to carry the weight of the world in your muscles and bones. Let go, let go, let go.” Song Circle song.

My shoulders hurt. My neck hurts too. As I get older, I’m increasingly unable to hold my head on top of my neck and it juts forward as my shoulders and upper back curl protectively around my heart. When I become aware of it, I can still tuck my chin in and rotate my shoulders back, but as soon as I’m not paying attention, my body resumes the shape of a question mark. I have done physical therapy, yoga and more chiropractic, cranial sacral and osteopathy that I can count (or afford!) Yet the pattern persists. When I tune into the deeper layers and the shape my body defaults into, this goes beyond mere structure and so it doesn’t yield to my willful attempts to straighten it out.

If I put my hand on my heart with a genuine curiosity to connect with what is here, I discover a part of me laboring under a heavy load, too heavy to remain upright. This part is many ages, starting in childhood when I felt my value to be dependent upon being useful to others and trying to carry their burdens for them.

So the question mark of my body is asking, “Will you love me for myself or only for my utility to you?” And “Am I lovable standing unapologetically in my messy, fucked up, unadorned humanness?” Most days, I don’t trust the answer.

Most days, uncurling from the protective shape that defends my open heart feels too raw and tender to bear. And so I practice being and communicating from an undefended place. And then I practice relaxing into things being exactly as they are - the defended and the undefended, the grief and the gratitude, the expansion and the contraction, both here - often simultaneously. It’s challenging, but compelling even so. Occasionally, on a good day, I feel something bigger, vast even, holding it all with infinite compassion.

In community and at events, when I’m communicating this way, I’m frequently  misunderstood as being “negative” or as someone who needs to be fixed. I’m clearly failing at the “#Living my best life” sweepstakes, and many people are happy to point that out to me. I just thank them for sharing and try to give them a hug. I know how exhausting it can be to have to keep scaling the heights at the expense of the depths!

My dad (and my first few husbands - don’t ask, it’s a long story!) had the opposite body shape. Dad especially, was stocky, with short legs, a long torso, and a broad chest and shoulders. He led with his chest, even though he was a very head-y guy. It was a power move. Puffed up, he could muscle in with a shoulder if he needed to.  As a trial lawyer, he was always facing down the opposition and needing to look bigger and meaner. His body was saying, “Pay attention, I am the most important, powerful man in the room. F\*#k with me at your peril.” Yet underneath that was, “I will never be vulnerable because vulnerability is too dangerous, and humans are not to be trusted.” 

My body shaped in the negative space in response to his push. I was his black mirror, taking on his disowned vulnerability and becoming the yield to his lack of flexibility. His power principle got pushed into my shadow. I became the yin to his yang, very sweet and compliant in my youth with a simmering dis-owned power charge underneath that would frequently get me into trouble. “What, who me? But I’m sooo nice!”

What shapes do you notice in the bodies around you? In your body? Do you lead with your head or your chest when you walk into a room? Have you noticed that some people lead with their pelvis, or parts in between? Are your eyes cast down at the ground, focused directly ahead, or at some point above and beyond? It’s so fascinating what we are communicating unconsciously with our body shapes, gazes and postures!
